Restaurants and grease

Food-service drains take a daily beating. Fats and oils cool in the pipe and harden, and without regular jetting the line narrows until it backs up at the worst moment. We schedule periodic jetting and camera checks, and we advise on grease interceptor care.

We also work with kitchen staff on what should never go down a floor drain, because the cheapest fix is usually prevention.

After-hours commercial plumbing emergencies

Protect the business while you wait

.

A burst line or sewage backup at a business is both a safety issue and a revenue issue. Customers and staff need a safe space, and inventory needs protecting.

  1. Close the main water valve and the affected fixture shutoff.
  2. Switch off power to wet areas at the panel if it is safe to reach.
  3. Move inventory and records away from water.
  4. Rope off any slippery or contaminated areas and keep customers out.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us the building type, address and what is affected.

Document the damage with photos and call your insurer promptly.

Landlords and property managers

Tenants call about plumbing at all hours, and managers need a plumber who answers. We coordinate access with tenants, send a written summary after each call, and give a clear price before work starts.

For apartment buildings, we trace leaks between units and check whether a problem is in one unit or in a shared line. Repeated calls on the same line are a sign to look at repiping or sewer repair.

Planned work: backflow and grease traps

Some commercial buildings need backflow device testing or grease trap service on a schedule. These are planned jobs, not emergencies. Ask us about timing and what your local utility requires. Requirements differ by account and use, so confirm with the utility before relying on any schedule.

What to have ready when you call for a business

Details help us send the right plumber with the right equipment.

  • The business name, address and the best contact on site.
  • Whether you own or lease, and who approves repairs.
  • What you see: where water is, what is not working and since when.
  • Whether the water is off and where the shutoff is.
  • Any compliance or health department deadline that affects the repair.

Why timing matters for a business

A residential leak is a repair. A leak in a restaurant or an office is also lost revenue. Every hour a kitchen is closed costs money, and a flooded floor can bring a health inspector. That is why we treat after-hours business calls as urgent and give a realistic arrival window on the phone.

Where we can, we stabilize the problem first and plan the full repair around your hours. A temporary fix that lets you open tomorrow is often the right first step, with the permanent repair scheduled when it costs you less.

Commercial codes and who inspects

Commercial plumbing follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC) as adopted in South Carolina, and the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation licenses contractors for commercial work over 10,000 dollars through its Contractors' Licensing Board. We tell you which rules apply to your building before we start.
