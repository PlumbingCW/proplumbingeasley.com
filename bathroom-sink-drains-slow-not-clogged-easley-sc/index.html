Where the buildup hides in a bathroom sink

Bathroom sinks drain through a small opening with a pop-up stopper on a rod. Hair wraps around the rod and the stopper's base, and soap and toothpaste glue it into a mat. The opening may look clear from above while the underside is almost closed.

The tailpiece, the short vertical pipe under the sink, can also collect scum. The P-trap catches whatever gets past, and then slow drainage follows.

Work from the top down.

How to clean a pop-up stopper

Most stoppers come out in a minute or two.

  1. Look under the sink for the pivot rod and the nut that holds it.
  2. Unscrew the nut and slide the rod out of the stopper.
  3. Lift the stopper out of the drain.
  4. Remove the hair and scrape off the film.
  5. Reinstall the stopper and rod, and check that it opens and closes.
  6. Run hot water and watch the flow.

When the sink is still slow after cleaning

If the stopper and trap are clean and the sink is still slow, the problem is further down. A partial blockage in the branch line or a vent problem can slow water. Test whether the bathtub and toilet are also slow. If they are, call for drain cleaning. If only the sink is slow, check the trap arm for a downward slope toward the wall.

A trap arm that tilts the wrong way can hold water and scum. A plumber can correct it in a short visit.

Vent clues in a bathroom

Gurgling when the sink drains, or a toilet that bubbles when the sink empties, points to a vent. A bathroom added in a remodel may share a vent with a nearby fixture that is too small or blocked. See plumbing installation for how new venting is planned.

Avoid chemical drain openers in a bathroom sink. They can damage plastic parts and are dangerous if they splash when the trap is opened.

Pop-up stoppers that stick or do not seal

A stopper that does not lift fully can restrict flow even when the drain is clear. The pivot rod may have slipped out of the clevis strap, or the stopper may be catching on the drain wall. Open the cabinet and watch the mechanism while someone works the lever.

If the stopper only lifts a quarter inch, loosen the nut on the pivot rod and reset the strap in a higher hole. That often restores full flow in minutes. A stopper that will not seal can be a problem in the other direction, with water leaking out of the basin, and the same adjustment fixes it.

If the stopper body is cracked or corroded, replace it. Match the finish and size, and bring the old one to the store.

Things not to do to a slow bathroom sink

These shortcuts make it worse.

  • Pour chemical openers into a sink that may need a trap cleaning.
  • Force a metal snake through a plastic trap or pop-up assembly.
  • Ignore a slow drain that is getting slower every week.
  • Remove the trap without a bucket underneath.
  • Use a plunger on a sink with the overflow open and not blocked.

What the plumbing code says about sink traps

The IPC sets trap size and the allowed length of the trap arm for a lavatory, and South Carolina adopts it with state modifications. A trap arm that slopes the wrong way can hold water and scum.

What this means in Easley, SC

Easley water is soft, with average hardness of 6 mg/L in the 2023 utility report. Soft water lathers readily, which means more soap is often used, and soap scum can build up faster in the trap and stopper than in a hard-water home. Cleaning the stopper every few months is a good habit here.

In older Easley houses near downtown, bathroom drains often connect to older cast iron or galvanized lines, which scale on the inside. A slow bathroom sink there may point to the branch line. In newer subdivisions, the cause is more often hair and a stopper.
