Symptoms that tell us where to look

Most water heater calls start with a symptom, not a part name. This is how we read them.

  • No hot water at all: tripped breaker, failed heating element, dead thermostat or a gas control problem.
  • Lukewarm water that runs out fast: a burned-out lower element or a tank full of sediment.
  • Water around the base: a loose fitting, a failing relief valve or a tank that has rusted through.
  • Popping or rumbling: sediment hardening on the tank floor.
  • Rust-colored hot water: a corroding tank or a depleted anode rod.

Water heater leaks: what to check first

Where the water appears matters. A drip at a fitting on top is a cheap fix. Water from the relief valve discharge pipe can mean excess pressure, not a bad tank. Water seeping from the bottom seam means the steel has failed and the tank needs replacing.

Many heaters sit in a garage or utility closet in this area, where a slow leak can run for weeks unseen. Check the floor around yours twice a year. If a tank is leaking from its body, see water heater replacement for what comes next.

Gas, electric and hybrid units

Gas heaters add combustion air, venting and gas supply to the checklist. We test for proper draft and look for scorching around the burner before we call a repair finished. Electric units rely on two elements and two thermostats, and we test each with a meter.

Hybrid heat pump heaters are growing in popularity. They need clearance and a condensate drain, and their repair is different from a standard tank. Tell us the brand on the label when you call so we can plan the visit.

Sediment, anode rods and what the local water does

Easley Combined Utilities draws from Saluda Lake and reported an average hardness of 6 mg/L in its 2023 water quality report. That is very soft, so heavy mineral scale is less likely here than in hard-water regions. Sediment can still collect over the years, and the anode rod still wears down.

What that means for you: flushing the tank every year or two and checking the anode rod can extend a heater's life, but you should not expect the scale problems that hard-water areas see.

When a water heater problem is an emergency

Steps to take before the plumber arrives

.

It is an emergency when the tank is actively leaking, when you smell gas near the heater, or when the relief valve is discharging hot water without stopping.

  1. Gas smell: leave the house, avoid switches, and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.
  2. Active leak: close the cold water inlet valve on the pipe above the heater.
  3. Turn the electric breaker off, or set a gas heater's control to off.
  4. Put a bucket under the leak and move stored items away from the floor.
  5. Do not touch the relief valve discharge pipe, because the water can be scalding.

If the heater is in a finished space, tell us so we can bring drain and wet-vac gear on the first visit.

What to check before you call

A few quick looks can save a visit or help us help you faster.

  • Is the breaker on, or is the pilot light lit on a gas unit?
  • Is there water on the floor, and where is it coming from?
  • Is the water lukewarm, scalding or cold?
  • Do you hear popping or rumbling from the tank?
  • How old is the heater? The label shows the date.

Do not relight a gas unit if you smell gas. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first.

Same-day repairs and what makes them possible

Most repairs use common parts, so many are finished on the first visit. Our plumbers carry elements, thermostats, relief valves and common gas controls on the truck. A part that has to be ordered adds a day, and we tell you so before leaving.

If the heater is old and the repair is major, we say that plainly. Putting money into a tank that is near the end of its life rarely makes sense. The honest answer is often to replace it, which our water heater replacement page explains.

Standards behind water heater safety parts

Gas storage water heaters are built to ANSI Z21.10.1, and the temperature and pressure relief valve on a tank is listed to ANSI Z21.22. We replace those parts with listed equivalents, and we never cap or reduce a relief valve discharge line.
