How to Repipe a House on a Slab: An Easley, SC Homeowner Guide

.

Repiping a house on a slab usually means abandoning the old lines under the concrete and running new pipe through the attic and walls. That avoids breaking the floor, which is the largest cost in any slab job.

This guide explains the method, the materials and the process for an Easley, SC home.

Why repipe a slab house

A slab house can have supply lines under the floor, and when those pipes start to fail the repairs are costly. If you have had more than one leak, if the water is rusty or discolored or if pressure keeps dropping, a repipe replaces the failing lines with new ones.

The goal is to remove the old slab lines from service and run new lines where they are easy to reach. That keeps the concrete intact and makes future repairs simpler.

Pipe materials used in repipes

The right one depends on the house, the budget and your preferences.

Material.Strengths.Trade-offs.
PEX.Flexible, fewer fittings, quicker install.Not for exposed outdoor sun and some codes limit use.
Copper.Long history and durable.Higher material cost, joints are soldered.
CPVC.Lower cost.Can become brittle with age and heat.
Galvanized (old).Found in older houses.Rusts inside, so it is usually the pipe being replaced.

How a slab house repipe goes

The sequence limits the time without water.

  1. Survey the house, find the main, the water heater and every fixture.
  2. Plan new routes through the attic and interior walls.
  3. Install a new manifold or distribution points.
  4. Open small access holes in drywall and run the new lines.
  5. Connect each fixture and test the system under pressure.
  6. Cap or abandon the old slab lines, patch the drywall and clean up.

Timeline and living in the house

A typical repipe takes one to three days depending on house size and access. The water is shut off for part of each day, and we tell you the schedule in advance. You can stay home, but you will want to plan showers and meals around the shutoff.

Drywall patching and painting are usually separate trades. We cut access holes carefully and tell you what to expect so you can schedule a patch and paint.

When a repair is better than a repipe

If a single, isolated leak has appeared in otherwise good pipe, a spot repair may be the better choice. A repipe makes sense when leaks repeat, when the pipe material is known to fail or when water quality drops because of corrosion inside the pipe.

Ask for both options and compare. Our repiping page covers the decision, and our slab leak repair page covers fixing a single leak.

Preparing your house for a repipe

A little preparation makes the job faster and less stressful. Clear the areas around each fixture, the water heater and the attic access. Move furniture away from walls where we expect to cut access holes, and cover valuables with sheets.

Plan for the water to be off for part of each day. Fill a few containers for drinking and cooking, and keep a roll of paper towels nearby. If you have pets, plan where they will stay while crews move through the house.

After the new lines are in, run each fixture to flush debris. Open each faucet for a minute, starting with the one closest to the main. Check the aerators, since small particles can collect there in the first few days.

Questions to ask before you hire for a repipe

A repipe is a large job, so compare more than the price.

  • Which pipe material do you recommend and why?
  • Will you leave the old slab lines in place or cap them?
  • Who patches and paints the drywall after the work?
  • How many days will the water be off and for how long each day?
  • What is the warranty on the pipe and on the labor?

Standards for repipe materials

PEX is made to ASTM F876, copper water tube to ASTM B88 and drinking water components should carry an NSF/ANSI 61 listing. A repipe follows the IPC as adopted in South Carolina.

What this means in Easley, SC

In Easley, many slab houses are in newer subdivisions and many older houses sit on crawlspaces. A crawlspace house is generally easier and cheaper to repipe. If you are not sure which you have, tell us when you call and we will ask a few questions to confirm.

Easley Combined Utilities supplies soft water, with a 2023 hardness average of 6 mg/L, so the water itself is not a common cause of pipe failure here. Age, movement and installation quality matter more. Ask the City of Easley building department whether your address needs a permit, and verify that your plumber holds a South Carolina license for residential work above 500 dollars.
