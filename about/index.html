What we do

We repair and install plumbing in houses and small commercial buildings. Our work includes drain cleaning, water heater repair, leak detection, sewer line repair and the other services on our services page.

Most of our calls are urgent. Someone has water on the floor, a drain that will not clear or a tank that has stopped making hot water. We built the phone line, the dispatch process and the pricing around that fact.

How a call works

The process is the same at any hour.

  1. You call (855) 575-2890. A person answers, day or night.
  2. You describe the problem and give your address. We tell you what to shut off or stop using.
  3. We send a plumber and give you an arrival window.
  4. The plumber finds the cause and gives you the price in writing before any work starts.
  5. You approve the work, or you do not. Either way you know the cost first.

Why we are based in Easley

Easley is a city of about 23,000 people in the southeastern corner of Pickens County, with a small part in Anderson County. The city grew around a railroad and textile mills, and its older streets sit beside newer subdivisions. That mix gives us the full range of plumbing work in one place.

From our Ross Avenue address we reach the nearby towns of Powdersville, Pickens, Liberty and Central. Easley Combined Utilities supplies the city's water from Saluda Lake, and its 2023 report puts average hardness at 6 mg/L, which is soft. Soft water is easy on fixtures and water heaters.

Licensing and standards

South Carolina requires a license for residential plumbing work that costs more than 500 dollars. The Residential Builders Commission, part of the Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation, issues those licenses. You can look up any plumber on the state's license lookup, and we encourage you to check anyone you hire, including us.

Our work follows the plumbing code that applies where you live. We pull a permit when the job calls for one, and we tell you who inspects it.

Emergency calls and planned work

Our phone line serves two kinds of callers. Some have water moving through a ceiling or a drain that will not clear, and they need a plumber the same day. Others are planning a remodel, a new water heater or a repipe, and they want a clear scope and a schedule. Both get the same written price before work begins.

For urgent calls, the dispatcher asks what you see, tells you what to shut off and sends the nearest available plumber. For planned work, we schedule a visit, walk the job and put the scope in writing. If a planned job turns up an urgent problem, we tell you which part cannot wait.

Easley's mix of older mill-era streets and newer subdivisions means no two houses are plumbed the same way. That is why we ask questions on the phone and look before we quote. A price given without seeing the problem is a guess, and we do not like guessing with your money.

What we do not do

We do not post fake reviews, invented awards or made-up statistics, and we do not promise an arrival time we cannot keep. We tell you the price before the work begins, and we explain what we found in plain words.

If a problem is outside our work, such as a gas leak, we tell you to call the gas utility or 911 first.
