Winter nights in Liberty

Most of the year Liberty's weather is mild, which is why a hard freeze catches so many houses off guard. Pipes in crawlspaces, exterior walls and unheated utility rooms are the first to freeze, and the damage often shows up only when the ice thaws and water starts to flow.

Liberty sits at about 984 feet, and the first cold morning of the season is a good time to check exposed pipes. Insulate supply lines under the house, disconnect garden hoses, and leave a faucet dripping on the coldest nights.

Mill-town housing and what is inside the walls

Liberty's first cotton mill opened in 1901, and a second followed in 1905. The streets built around them have small, older houses, and many have been renovated in pieces over the years. That usually means a mix of galvanized, copper and newer plastic pipe in one house.

Mixed materials fail at the joints where they meet. If you have rusty water, low pressure or repeated small leaks, see our repiping page. A planned repipe costs more up front than a patch, and it replaces many failure points at once.

Water and local facts

The City of Liberty buys its water as treated surface water from the Greenville Water System and the Easley-Central Water Department. Its 2024 water quality report shows copper well under the action level and lead at zero for the 90th percentile in the sampling round. That is good news, but household pipe condition still affects what comes out of your tap.

A utility report tells you about the water at the plant. It does not tell you about the pipe inside your wall. If water is discolored, leak detection and a pipe check tell you whether the problem is yours.

Getting around Liberty

US 178 runs through Liberty, and we use it as a reference point with callers. The city's old mill sites, the Sarlin Community Library and Liberty Presbyterian Church, built in 1883, are also familiar markers. Easley is about 8 miles east, so a call from Liberty is a short trip.

The Liberty city center sits close to the Easley line, and we also cover the roads out toward Pickens and Central.

Liberty homeowner checklist

A small list before the season changes keeps pipes working.

  • Photograph your crawlspace pipes so you know what you have.
  • Test your main shutoff and your outdoor faucet shutoffs each fall.
  • Replace brittle supply lines under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Flush the water heater once a year to clear sediment.
  • Keep our number near the breaker panel with the other emergency contacts.
