What hydro jetting actually does

A hydro jetter uses a nozzle that fires water jets backward and forward as it moves down the line. The backward jets pull the hose forward and the forward jets break up the blockage. The water then scours the pipe wall as the nozzle is drawn back.

A cable punches a hole through a clog. A jetter cleans the pipe. When grease, scale or fine roots coat the wall, jetting is what keeps the line from clogging again in a few weeks.

When jetting is the better tool

We recommend jetting when the symptoms match these patterns.

  • A line was snaked recently and clogged again within weeks.
  • A cable stuck or could not pass a certain distance.
  • The camera shows grease, scale or roots coating the pipe wall.
  • A restaurant or shop floor drain keeps backing up.
  • A main sewer line slows every fixture in the house.

When jetting is not the answer

Jetting cannot fix a broken pipe, and high pressure in a cracked or collapsed line can make things worse. That is why we run a camera first on any line that has had repeated trouble.

If the camera shows a crack, an offset joint or a belly where water sits, the right next step is sewer line repair, not more cleaning.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Protect the house and your family

.

A backed-up main line sends wastewater out of the lowest drain in the house. This is an emergency because sewage damages floors and is a health risk.

  1. Stop all water use, including toilets, dishwashers and washing machines.
  2. Keep children and pets away from the affected area.
  3. Do not use chemical drain cleaners on a fully blocked main line.
  4. Open a window for ventilation and wear gloves if you must touch anything.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and say whether sewage has reached the floor.

If a neighbor's yard shows similar backing up, the blockage may be in the city's main. Tell us and we will help you work out who to call.

Restaurants, shops and multi-unit buildings

Commercial kitchens send grease into drain lines every day. Without regular jetting, those lines narrow until the first slow Friday night turns into an overflow. We schedule jetting for food-service and multi-unit properties, which our commercial plumbing page covers.

What a jetting visit includes

The process is more careful than the pressure suggests.

  1. A camera look first, so we know what the jet will meet.
  2. A cleanout or access point opened and the line protected.
  3. The jet nozzle run through the line at controlled pressure.
  4. A second camera pass to confirm the pipe wall is clear.
  5. A short summary of what we found and whether a repair is needed.

Risks and how we manage them

High-pressure water is not right for every pipe. A fragile or collapsed line can be damaged by the jet, which is why we look with a camera first. We choose the nozzle and pressure to match the pipe material and condition, and we stop if the camera shows a defect that jetting could worsen.

On older clay or cast iron lines, we favor a gentler approach when the pipe wall looks thin. In many cases a cable clears the immediate blockage, and a repair follows later. We explain the risk before we decide.

Why a camera check matches the pipe to the method

Pipe materials have published standards of their own, such as ASTM A888 and CISPI 301 for hubless cast iron soil pipe. A line built to those standards can usually take jetting, while a thin, scaled or cracked wall may not, which is why we look first and choose the nozzle second.
