The meter test: is water leaking right now?

Many leak calls begin with a number, not a puddle. The water bill jumped, or the meter dial moves when nobody is home. You can check this yourself. Turn off every faucet and appliance, then watch the small leak indicator on your water meter for a few minutes.

If it moves, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. That could be a toilet flapper, an irrigation line, a pipe in the wall or a buried service line. The next step is working out which.

Symptoms that point to a hidden leak

You do not need to see water to have a leak. These signs show up first.

  • A water bill that rises without a change in how you live.
  • The meter dial turning with all water off.
  • Warm spots on a floor, which can mean a hot line leaking under a slab.
  • Musty smells, bubbling paint or stained ceilings and walls.
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on.
  • Low water pressure that appears suddenly.

Ceiling and wall leaks

A stain on a ceiling rarely sits under its source. Water travels along joists and pipes before it drips. We trace upward from the stain, checking the bathroom above, the supply lines, the drain and the roof penetrations that sometimes get blamed on plumbing.

If the stain is under a bathroom or kitchen, a leaking shower pan, tub drain or supply line is the usual suspect. If it is under an attic, a water heater or condensate line may be responsible.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks

Many Upstate homes have crawlspaces where pipes hang exposed. A slow drip can soak insulation, rot a subfloor and feed mold for months. Signs include cupped floorboards, a musty smell and a meter that moves.

We check the supply lines and drains under the house, and we tell you what we see. If a pipe is corroded in several places, replacing the run may be wiser than patching, which our repiping team can explain.

When a leak is an emergency

Steps to take while you wait

.

A leak is an emergency when water is flowing steadily, when it touches electrical outlets or the panel, or when a ceiling is bulging with trapped water.

  1. Close the main water shutoff valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to the wet area at the breaker if you can do so safely and dry-handed.
  3. Place a bucket under any drip, and for a bulging ceiling, ask us before puncturing it.
  4. Move furniture and electronics away from the wet zone.
  5. Photograph the damage for insurance and call the 24/7 line.

If you cannot find the shutoff, tell us your address and we will guide you.

Slab, yard and irrigation leaks

Slab leaks are covered on our slab leak repair page. Buried lines between the meter and the house are a different job, covered by water line repair.

Irrigation lines and outdoor meter-box leaks also drive high bills. We check the meter box, the hose bibs and the irrigation manifold as part of a high-bill call. Pool and spa leaks are possible to trace, but ask us about your setup before booking.

What to document for an insurance claim

A clear record helps if the leak damaged anything.

  • Photos of the damage before anything is moved.
  • The date you first noticed the problem and the meter reading.
  • The plumber's written findings and the location of the leak.
  • Receipts for any emergency repairs or drying equipment.
  • A list of damaged items, with approximate age and value.

A high bill is not always a leak

Before we look for a hidden leak, we rule out the easy causes. A toilet flapper that does not seal can waste hundreds of gallons a day without making a sound. An irrigation zone left running or a hose bib that drips adds up as well. A seasonal change in use, like filling a pool, can also lift a bill.

That is why the meter test comes first. If the meter moves with every fixture off, a leak exists. If it does not, the bill reflects use. We explain which one it is on the call and only send a plumber when it makes sense.

Guidance on drying and mold after a leak

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency guidelines advise that wet building materials be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. That is why we write down where we found moisture, so a drying contractor and your insurer know exactly where to look.
