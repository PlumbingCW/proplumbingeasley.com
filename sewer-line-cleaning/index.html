What blocks a main sewer line

Main lines carry everything the house drains. The most common blockages are flushed wipes and paper, kitchen grease, roots and in older lines, scale and sag. Each behaves differently, so the tool matters.

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down like toilet paper. They catch on rough joints and gather into a mass that stops the line.

Main line backing up: what to do first

Steps before the plumber arrives

.

A main line backup sends sewage out of the lowest drain. It is both a health risk and fast-moving property damage.

  1. Stop all water use.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the affected area.
  3. Avoid chemical cleaners and plunging a full main line.
  4. Open windows and wear gloves for any contact.
  5. Call the 24/7 line, and note which drain is overflowing.

If you have a basement or low level, tell us the lowest drain location. It often shows where the blockage is relative to the main.

How we keep a main line from clogging again

Cleaning is half the answer. These habits protect the line.

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste.
  • Keep grease out of kitchen drains, and wipe pans before washing.
  • Install a drain strainer in tubs and showers.
  • Have a camera look at the line if clogs return within a few months.
  • Trim or remove tree roots near the line before they reach it.

Newer subdivisions and older neighborhoods

Newer homes in the growing area south of Easley often have PVC lines and fewer joints. Builders' debris can cause early trouble, and settled soil can create bellies. Older homes closer to downtown may have cast iron or clay that traps roots.

We see both and adapt. If the camera reveals structural damage, see sewer line repair for next steps.

Signs a main line is partly blocked

Catch it early and you avoid the mess.

  • Slow drains in several fixtures at once.
  • Gurgling in a toilet or a floor drain when the washer runs.
  • A sewer smell near a cleanout or a basement drain.
  • Water backing up in the lowest fixture in the house.
  • Frequent clogs that return after a cleaning.

What a cleanout does for you

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main line. With one, a plumber can clean the line in a short visit. Without one, access may be through a toilet or a roof vent, which takes longer and risks damage.

If your house has no cleanout, installing one is a small project that pays off every time the line needs service. Ask us about adding one when we clean the line.

A cleanout should be capped and clear of mulch or soil so it can be found in an emergency. Mark its location with a stake or a note in your home records.

What to do before help arrives, and the rules behind it

Do not run water into a backed-up line, and keep children and pets away from sewage. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention both advise limiting contact with sewage and washing hands after any exposure.
