How we decide a replacement is the right call

A heater that leaks from the tank body cannot be repaired, and one that is over a decade old with repeat failures is usually past its useful life. We look at the tank age on the label, the leak location, the venting and the supply lines before we recommend anything.

If a repair makes more sense, we say so. Our water heater repair page explains which problems are worth fixing.

Sizing the new heater for your household

Tank size follows how many people shower and how many fixtures run at once. A couple in a small house may do well with 40 gallons. A family of four with a soaking tub often needs 50 or more.

Bigger is not always better. A tank larger than your demand wastes energy keeping extra water hot, and it may not fit the space or the existing venting.

What the replacement visit includes

Every job follows the same safety steps, whatever the brand.

  • Shutting off water, power or gas and draining the old tank.
  • Disconnecting and hauling away the old heater.
  • Setting the new unit and connecting new flex lines where needed.
  • Checking the relief valve, discharge line and expansion tank.
  • Testing for leaks, temperature and, on gas units, draft and flame.
  • Explaining the settings and the date to flush the tank next.

Tank failure: what to do in the first ten minutes

Limit the damage

.

A tank that has split can release forty or fifty gallons across a floor. Speed matters most in the first few minutes.

  1. Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the heater.
  2. Switch off the breaker or turn the gas control to off.
  3. Move anything valuable off the floor and use towels to direct water to a drain.
  4. Open the lowest hose bib or drain valve if it is safe to reach.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us the tank size and fuel type.

If water has soaked drywall or flooring, photograph it before cleanup and contact your insurer as soon as practical.

Permits, licensing and doing it right

Residential plumbing work above 500 dollars in South Carolina requires a license under the state's Residential Builders Commission. You can verify a plumber's license on the South Carolina Labor, Licensing and Regulation lookup tool before you let anyone start.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Easley building department whether your replacement needs one. We will tell you what we know about your address.

Before the old tank comes out

A few minutes of prep makes the day go faster.

  • Clear a path to the heater and the area around it.
  • Tell us where the water and gas shutoffs are, or ask us to find them.
  • Plan for no hot water for several hours.
  • Decide whether you want a different size or fuel, and tell us before we quote.
  • Keep pets out of the work area.

Why same-day replacement is a real option

When a tank fails at night, hot water is the least of the worries. The leak can damage floors and walls in hours. We stop the water, drain the tank and tell you what a replacement involves right then, so the decision does not wait for a second visit.

If the right unit is in stock, we can often install it the same day. If it needs a special vent part or a larger size, we say so up front and give you a realistic window. Either way, the price is in writing before the old tank is touched.

If you are choosing between a like-for-like swap and a new type of heater, our water heater installation and tankless water heater pages explain the differences.

Codes and standards for a replacement

A new tank must meet the manufacturer's installation instructions and the IPC as adopted in South Carolina. Gas units follow ANSI Z21.10.1 and tankless gas units ANSI Z21.10.3, which is why venting and gas line size get checked before the old tank comes out.
