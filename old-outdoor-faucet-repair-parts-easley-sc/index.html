An old outdoor faucet usually fails in one of a few parts: the packing washer around the stem, the stem washer inside, the handle or the valve seat. Most drips can be fixed with a part that costs a few dollars.

How an outdoor faucet works

A standard hose bib has a handle, a stem, a packing nut and washer around the stem, and a washer at the bottom that closes against a seat. When you turn the handle, the stem lifts the washer and water flows. Wear and mineral buildup cause the usual leaks.

A frost-proof sillcock puts the valve deep inside the wall so the water shuts off where it will not freeze. If it is installed on a slope and a hose is left on, it can still freeze.

What leaks and which part fixes it

Match the leak to the part.

Steps to replace a stem washer

This is a simple repair for an accessible faucet.

  1. Shut off the water to the faucet at the interior valve, or at the main.
  2. Open the faucet to drain the line.
  3. Remove the handle screw and handle.
  4. Unscrew the packing nut and pull out the stem.
  5. Replace the washer and the packing with matching parts.
  6. Reassemble, turn the water on slowly and check for leaks.

When to replace the whole faucet

Parts for very old faucets are sometimes hard to find. If the body is cracked, the seat is pitted or the stem threads are stripped, replace the spigot. A new frost-proof unit with a built-in vacuum breaker is a better choice for most houses.

If water is leaking inside the wall, shut off the supply and call us. That is a case for leak detection and, if the pipe has failed, faucet repair and repair of the line behind it.

Winter prep for outdoor faucets

Disconnect the hose before the first hard freeze. A hose left on traps water in the faucet and can split it. If your house has a shutoff valve for the outdoor line, close it and open the faucet to drain it.

A cover helps in a hard freeze. If a pipe does burst, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line. Our burst pipe repair page walks through the steps.

Finding parts for an old brass faucet

Start by removing the handle and stem and taking them to a hardware store. Match the stem length, thread count and the washer size. A stem that looks the same can differ by a few threads, and a washer that is slightly off will leak again in a week.

If the faucet is stamped with a maker name, a replacement stem kit may be available. If not, a universal repair kit with several washer sizes and a few packing washers usually covers it. Keep the kit in a drawer, because the same parts fit other hose bibs.

If the seat is scored, you can sometimes dress it with a seat tool. If it is too pitted, replacing the spigot is cleaner than chasing a leak. A new sillcock with a vacuum breaker also protects the house water from backflow if a hose is left in a bucket.

Tools and parts to have on hand

Most repairs need only a few items.

  • Adjustable wrench and a screwdriver for the handle screw.
  • Replacement stem washers in several sizes.
  • Packing washers or packing string for the stem.
  • Plumber's grease for the threads.
  • A new hose washer, which is cheap and often the real culprit.

What to do if the faucet will not shut off, and the standard behind the fix

If a faucet will not stop, close the supply valve inside the house, or the main valve if needed, and do not force the stem. Hose connection vacuum breakers are made to ASSE 1011, which protects household water from backflow.

What this means in Easley, SC

Upstate South Carolina has mild winters, but hard freezes happen, and outdoor faucets on north-facing walls are the first to be hit. A leak inside a wall from a frozen faucet may not show until the thaw. Check inside the wall and the crawlspace after a freeze.

Easley's soft water, with average hardness at 6 mg/L in the 2023 utility report, means less mineral buildup on the stem and seat than in hard-water areas, so a washer usually fails from age, not scale.
