On Demand Water Heater Installation: Easley, SC Homeowner Guide

.

An on demand water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so there is no tank to keep warm and no tank to fail. Installation is more involved than a tank swap, because the unit needs the right gas line, venting or electrical service.

This guide walks through the decision for a house in Easley, SC, from sizing to the work a plumber does.

How an on demand unit works

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor starts the burner or the elements. Water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves hot, and when you close the tap the unit shuts off. You never run out of hot water in the way a tank can, but you are limited by how many gallons per minute the unit can heat.

That limit is the key to sizing. A shower, a dishwasher and a washer running at once can exceed a small unit. A larger unit or two units in a larger house solves it.

Gas versus electric on demand units

The fuel choice changes the installation.

Feature.Gas.Electric.
Output.Higher flow for the size.Lower flow, often for one bathroom or a point of use.
Installation.Gas line, venting and a condensate drain on some models.Heavy electrical circuit and breaker capacity.
Best fit.Whole houses.Small houses, additions and remote fixtures.
Watch for.Gas line size and vent route.Panel capacity.

What a plumber checks before installation

The site decides whether the unit fits.

  • Number of bathrooms and the largest hot-water draw at one time.
  • Incoming water temperature in winter, which lowers flow.
  • Gas line size and meter capacity, or panel capacity for electric.
  • Vent path to the outside and clearances.
  • Wall space, drain and where a pressure relief line can run.

Water quality and maintenance

Hard water scales the heat exchanger of a tankless unit, which lowers flow and shortens life. Water in Easley is soft, which is a point in favor of a tankless unit here. Even so, an annual flush keeps the exchanger clean. Our tankless water heater page covers flushing and service.

If you choose a unit, plan for a service valve kit so flushing is easy. It is a small part that saves effort every year.

Replacing a tank with a tankless unit

A swap is not plug and play. A gas tank usually has a small gas line and a flue sized for the tank. A tankless unit may need a larger gas line and a different vent material. The extra work shows up as a higher installed price than the unit alone.

For a straight swap to another tank, see water heater replacement. For the full installation scope, see water heater installation.

Sizing by flow rate and temperature rise

Two numbers set the size of an on demand unit. The first is flow, measured in gallons per minute, and the second is temperature rise, the difference between the incoming water temperature and the temperature you want. Incoming water is colder in winter, so a unit that keeps up in July may fall short in January.

A typical shower uses around two gallons per minute, a kitchen faucet about one and a half, and a dishwasher or washer a little more than one. Add up the fixtures that run at the same time, then size the unit for that peak. A plumber can estimate it in a few minutes from a walk through the house.

If your peak demand is higher than one unit can handle, two units can be linked, or you can choose to stagger use. Most families find that one properly sized gas unit covers a house with two or three bathrooms.

Questions to ask any installer

A good installer answers these without hesitation.

  • What size gas line and vent material does this model need in my house?
  • Will you add service valves so the unit can be flushed?
  • What is the expected flow at my winter water temperature?
  • Does the quote include permit, condensate drain and haul-away?
  • What does the warranty cover on the heat exchanger and on labor?

Standards for gas and electric on demand units

Gas tankless units are certified to ANSI Z21.10.3, and installation must follow both the manufacturer's manual and the IPC as adopted in South Carolina. Electrical work follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) edition the state enforces.

What this means in Easley, SC

Easley Combined Utilities reports an average hardness of 6 mg/L and a pH near 7.2 in its 2023 water quality report, with water drawn from Saluda Lake. That is soft water, so scale is less of a worry for a heat exchanger here than in hard-water areas. Check your own utility if you live outside the city, since the Powdersville area uses a separate district.

Ask the City of Easley building department whether your address needs a permit for gas or electrical changes. If you live in the county, ask the county office. South Carolina requires a license for residential plumbing work over 500 dollars.
