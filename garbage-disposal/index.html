What each disposal symptom usually means

Symptoms tell us which part to check.

  • Hums but does not spin: a jam. The motor is trying to turn but something blocks the plate.
  • Silent with no hum: a tripped reset button or an electrical fault.
  • Leaks from the bottom: a cracked housing or failed seal, usually a replacement.
  • Leaks from the top: a loose sink flange or old plumber's putty.
  • Leaks from the side: a loose drain connection or dishwasher hose.

Clearing a jam safely

Cut power first by unplugging the unit or switching off its breaker. Many disposals have a hex socket on the bottom for an Allen wrench that turns the plate by hand. Work it back and forth until it spins freely, then press the red reset button.

If it jams again right away, there may be more stuck inside, or the motor may be failing. We can check and replace the unit if needed.

When a disposal is an emergency

Limit the water and the risk

.

A leak under the sink can soak the cabinet and the flooring beneath it. Any electrical exposure near water is a concern.

  1. Unplug the disposal or switch off its breaker before touching anything.
  2. Close the shutoff valves under the sink.
  3. Place a bowl or bucket under the leak.
  4. Empty the cabinet and dry the area.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us if the leak is at the top, side or bottom.

If water has reached outlets or the breaker box, stay clear and call us immediately.

Backing up into the other sink or the dishwasher

If the other sink basin or dishwasher backs up when the disposal runs, the drain line past the disposal is probably blocked. This is a drain issue, not a disposal issue. Our drain cleaning team clears kitchen lines with a cable or jetter.

Grease is the usual culprit, so avoid pouring it into the sink even with the disposal running.

Installing a new disposal

A new disposal needs a compatible sink flange, a proper drain connection and, if the dishwasher drains through it, an opened knockout plug. We handle the electrical connection if an outlet exists, and tell you if a new outlet or switch is needed.

Horsepower matters less than most people think. A one-half to three-quarter horsepower unit handles most households, and larger units cost more without a big gain.

Safety first when you reach for the unit

Never put a hand into a disposal, even if the switch is off. Turn off the wall switch and, if you can, the breaker, before you use the hex wrench or tongs. A unit that restarts while you are working can cause a serious injury.

If the disposal is jammed and the reset does not help, stop and call. A motor that has burned out will not respond to resets, and forcing it can damage the sink or the plumbing.

Safety and code points for disposals

The IPC requires an air gap or a high loop on a dishwasher drain, which is why we check that connection on every disposal job. Manufacturers also warn never to put a hand into a disposal, even when it is switched off.
