Toilet problems and their usual causes

Most toilet problems trace to a handful of parts.

  • Running after flush: a worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve or a bent chain.
  • Water at the base: a failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt.
  • Rocking toilet: a loose flange or damaged flooring.
  • Weak flush: a clogged rim jet or partial drain blockage.
  • Phantom flushing: a leaking flapper letting water slip into the bowl.

Leaking at the base: why it matters

Water seeping around a toilet base is rarely a small problem. It often comes from a wax ring that has failed, and it can rot the subfloor over months. The damage is hidden until the floor feels soft or a ceiling below stains.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and the floor, replace the ring and reseat it. If the flange is cracked, we repair it so the toilet sits level and stays sealed.

When a toilet problem is an emergency

Stop the water first

.

An overflowing toilet can put gallons on the floor in minutes. A leak through a ceiling below is worse.

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper closed, or close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  2. Do not flush again while the bowl is high.
  3. Use towels to limit spread and move rugs away.
  4. If the supply valve is stuck, close the main water valve.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us whether it is a clog or a leak.

If the toilet is on an upper floor, check the ceiling below for stains. We can help you decide how to handle it.

Installing a new toilet

A new toilet is more than swapping fixtures. We check the flange height, the shutoff valve, the supply line and the floor under the old unit. If any of those are worn, we replace them while the toilet is out.

Tall or comfort-height models, dual-flush units and one-piece designs all install differently. Tell us the model or the look you want and we confirm the rough-in size before we start.

When the problem is a clog, not a part

A toilet that will not flush may be blocked at the trap or at the drain line. A single clogged toilet is usually local. If several fixtures are slow, the blockage is in the branch line or the main, which our drain cleaning team handles.

Why a running toilet is expensive

A toilet that runs can waste hundreds of gallons a day without making a sound. The water goes straight down the drain, so it never shows as a puddle. It shows as a bill. A dye tablet in the tank, with no flush for ten minutes, tells you if the flapper leaks. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper needs replacing.

That is a simple repair, and it is one of the best-value jobs a plumber does. We also check the fill valve and the water level at the same visit.

Standards for toilet flush volume and parts

Federal law sets the maximum flush volume for toilets at 1.6 gallons, and many models use 1.28 gallons or less under EPA WaterSense. Replacement parts should match the fill valve and flapper the tank was designed for.
