Signs your sewer line needs repair, not cleaning

A clog and a damaged pipe can look alike at first. These clues suggest damage.

  • Backups that return after jetting or snaking.
  • Gurgling toilets and slow drains across several fixtures.
  • Wet or unusually green patches in the yard along the line's path.
  • Sewer odors outside near the cleanout or under the house.
  • Cracked slab or sinking soil above where the pipe runs.

What damages sewer lines in this area

Easley and the surrounding Upstate have mature trees, and roots look for the moisture that drains give off. A hairline joint or a hole the size of a pencil is enough for roots to enter and grow into a mat.

Age matters too. Older homes may have cast iron or clay lines that crack or corrode, while newer lines can be damaged by settling soil or a missed connection. A camera inspection shows which problem you have.

A localized crack with root intrusion

This is one of the most common calls we hear. A single crack lets roots in, and the homeowner wants to know whether the whole line must go. Often it does not.

We cut the roots, run the camera, and mark the damaged section. If the rest of the line is sound, a spot repair at that section can be the right fix. If the camera shows multiple faults, we discuss lining or replacement.

Sewer backup: what to do before we arrive

Protect people and property

.

Treat any sewage on the floor as an emergency and keep the area clear.

  1. Stop using water everywhere in the house.
  2. Keep children and pets out of the affected room.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the wet area.
  4. Ventilate with a window and wear gloves for any contact.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell us where the backup is showing.

If you can safely find your cleanout, show us where it is. It speeds up the camera work.

Where the city line ends and yours begins

Most municipal systems treat the lateral from the house to the main differently than the main itself. Easley Combined Utilities handles sewer service in the city, so ask it where its responsibility ends on your street.

That answer decides whether the repair is yours or the utility's. We can help document what the camera shows.

What a repair visit looks like

A repair starts with a camera and ends with a test.

  1. Camera inspection and a locator mark on the ground.
  2. A written scope and price for the chosen method.
  3. Call 811 and mark the utilities before any digging.
  4. Excavation or lining, depending on the plan.
  5. A final camera pass or flow test to confirm the repair.

Questions to ask about any sewer repair quote

Ask which method the quote assumes and why. Ask what happens to the driveway, the landscaping and any trees in the path. Ask who pulls the permit and who handles the inspection. Ask for a warranty in writing and what it covers.

A good quote names the footage, the depth and the pipe material. If those are missing, the number is a guess. We give the scope with the video in front of you so the price rests on what is in the ground.

Codes and one-call rules for sewer work

South Carolina adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the International Residential Code (IRC) through its Building Codes Council, with state modifications. The edition in force is listed on the council's page at the state Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. Before any digging, South Carolina law requires a call to the state's 811 service so utilities are marked. We handle that call and the permit question as part of the repair plan.
