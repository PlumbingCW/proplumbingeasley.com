Why hot water lines leak under slabs

Hot lines expand and contract every time they heat and cool. Under a slab, that movement can rub the pipe against concrete or rock, and the pipe can thin until it pinholes. Copper lines are especially prone to this, and the leak often shows up first as a warm floor spot or a hot water bill that climbs.

Cold lines can leak too, but hot lines tend to fail first. If your water heater seems to run all the time or your hot water is slow to arrive, ask about a leak.

What the leak detection step includes

Finding the leak before cutting is what keeps the cost down.

  • Pressure test to confirm the leak and which line is affected.
  • Acoustic listening on the floor to narrow the area.
  • Thermal imaging or a line tracer to mark the path.
  • A short camera look in accessible areas.
  • A clear write-up of the findings and options.

Our leak detection page covers the tools. A slab leak adds the choice of repair method, which our slab leak repair page explains.

Why one repair can lead to another

If a pipe has worn through in one spot, the rest of that line was exposed to the same conditions. A single repair that holds for years is common, but a second leak in the same line a year later is also common. That is why a quote may include a reroute or repipe option alongside a spot repair.

A reroute costs more than a spot repair and less than a full demolition of the floor. It also takes the leaking line out of the slab for good. Ask for both prices and compare. See repiping if the whole house is a candidate.

Hidden costs to ask about

Plumbing is only one part of the bill. Floor finishes, tile matching, drywall, paint and drying can be separate. If a leak has run for weeks, you may have wet insulation or mold to deal with. Ask the plumber what is included and what is not.

If the leak has caused damage, photograph everything and call your insurer before work. Many policies cover the sudden damage but not the cost of reaching the pipe.

How to read a slab leak quote

A clear quote lists the leak detection step, the repair method and what is excluded. If one quote is much lower than another, check what is left out. Floor repair, drying and permit fees are the items most often missing.

Ask what happens if the opened section shows more damage than expected. A good plumber explains the plan for that case before work starts, so a surprise does not become a dispute. Also ask for a warranty on the repair and what it covers.

Be wary of any quote that is given before anyone has found the leak. Without a pressure test and a location, a price is a guess. We give the number after we know where the leak is and how we will reach it.

Steps to take while you wait for a plumber

Acting early limits the bill.

  • Close the main water valve and open a faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Turn off the water heater so the tank is not drained dry.
  • Take photos of any damp floor, stains or cracks.
  • Move rugs, boxes and furniture off the affected area.
  • Write down your meter reading and the time, so we can compare after the shutoff.

Sources for slab leak guidance

Drying follows the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency guidelines, which advise drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Insurance coverage varies by policy, so confirm with your insurer, and verify any plumber through the state license lookup.

What this means in Easley, SC

Not every Easley house sits on a slab. Many older homes near downtown have crawlspaces, and newer subdivisions often use slabs. Check your foundation first, since a crawlspace leak is much easier and cheaper to reach. Our plumber can confirm the foundation type on the phone from a few questions.

Easley water is soft, with average hardness of 6 mg/L in the 2023 utility report, and the utility adds polyphosphate for corrosion control. That is helpful, but pipes can still fail from wear and movement. If you see a warm spot, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line rather than waiting for a bill.
