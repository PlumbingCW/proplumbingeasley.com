Rough-in: the stage that decides everything

Rough-in is the plumbing that goes in before walls and floors close. Drain sizes, slopes, vents and supply routing all get set here. A mistake at this stage is expensive to fix later.

We review your plans, mark pipe locations and walk the job with your builder or tile installer so the layout matches the fixtures you chose.

Before we start: what to have ready

A little preparation makes the work faster.

  • Fixture models, or at least their rough-in dimensions.
  • A rough sketch or plans showing the layout.
  • Access to the crawlspace, attic or basement where lines will run.
  • Notes on any existing plumbing issues in the area.
  • The name of your builder or inspector if one is involved.

Licensing and permits

South Carolina requires a license for residential plumbing work above 500 dollars, issued through the Residential Builders Commission. You can look up any plumber's license on the Labor, Licensing and Regulation lookup tool.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Easley building department about your address. If your project is in an unincorporated area, the county handles permits. We help you figure out who to call.

Water pressure and local water

Easley Combined Utilities supplies treated surface water with average hardness of 6 mg/L and pH near 7.2 in its 2023 report. For new installs, that means fixtures are less likely to clog with scale than in hard-water areas.

We still check pressure at the house. High pressure can damage fixtures and appliances, and a pressure regulator is a simple fix when needed.

Questions to ask any plumber before a remodel

Good answers up front save problems later.

  • Who pulls the permit, and who schedules the inspection?
  • How will you coordinate with the tile installer and the builder?
  • What happens if the walls open and the old plumbing is worse than expected?
  • What is included in the price: fixtures, supply lines, shutoffs and disposal?
  • What is the warranty on the work?

Planning around the other trades

Plumbing sits in the middle of a remodel. The framer needs to know where the drains go before the walls close. The tile installer needs the valve set at the right depth. The electrician needs the water heater location for the circuit. A short planning call with each saves rework.

We walk the job with the other trades, mark the rough-in on the floor and confirm the fixture models before anything is cut. It is a small step that avoids a lot of arguments.

The codes a plumbing installation answers to

South Carolina adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the International Residential Code (IRC) through its Building Codes Council, with state modifications. The edition in force is listed on the council's page at the state Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation. Rough-in is where those rules apply most, which is why we review plans before walls close.
