What a camera inspection tells you

Pipes hide problems. A camera shows cracked or offset joints, roots, grease buildup, bellies where water sits, and sections of different pipe material. It also confirms whether a recurring clog is a cleaning issue or a repair issue.

That information saves money. Without it, repairs are guesses and cleaning can become a monthly habit.

When to book an inspection

Inspections are useful in more situations than a backup.

  • Buying an older home and wanting to know the condition of the main line.
  • A drain that keeps clogging after cleaning.
  • Before landscaping or building over a sewer path.
  • After a sewage backup, to find the cause.
  • Before a major renovation that adds fixtures to the line.

Inspecting before you buy a house

A general home inspection rarely includes the underground sewer line. In an older neighborhood, that line could be several decades old and made of cast iron, clay or early plastic.

A camera inspection during the due diligence period can reveal a six-figure surprise or a clean bill of health. Ask us for a written summary and a copy of the footage to share with your agent.

Finding where the line runs

If you do not know where your cleanout or main line sits, we can locate it as part of the inspection. A locator on the camera shows the pipe's path and depth from above ground, which helps with planning an addition or tree work.

What you receive after an inspection

We give you the video, a note of any defects and how far from the cleanout they sit, the pipe material we saw and the depth where the locator could read it. If the line is clean and sound, you get a clear statement of that. If we find a problem, we explain the options and the order in which to handle them.

A report is useful long after the visit. It tells you what to watch, helps another plumber plan a repair and gives a buyer or an insurer a record of the line's condition on that day.

Preparing for an inspection

A little preparation speeds up the visit.

  • Find your cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the yard or near the foundation.
  • Clear space around it so the equipment can be set up.
  • Run the washer or flush toilets only if we ask.
  • Tell us about past backups, roots or repairs.
  • Plan for about an hour, longer if the line is long or blocked.

Inspection versus cleaning

A cleaning clears a blockage. An inspection shows why the blockage happened. Many homeowners do a cleaning first and never see the pipe, then have the same problem again. If the line has backed up more than once, an inspection is the better value.

If the camera finds roots or scale, a cleaning can follow right away, and a repair can be priced from the video. See sewer line cleaning and sewer line repair for those steps.

Sources we use for inspection findings

Our findings use plain pipe terms such as crack, offset joint, root intrusion and belly. For the pipe itself we cite the material standards that apply, such as ASTM A888 for hubless cast iron and ASTM D3034 for PVC sewer pipe, so another plumber can read the report without guessing.
