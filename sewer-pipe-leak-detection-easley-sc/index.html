What a sewer leak is

A sewer line carries wastewater from your house to the city main or a septic tank. A leak lets wastewater escape into the soil, and it can also let roots and groundwater in. Cracks, separated joints and old pipe are the usual causes.

Because the line is underground, the leak can go on for months. The first sign is often a smell or a wet area, but by then the pipe may have been leaking for a long time.

Signs a sewer pipe may be leaking

No single sign proves a leak, but several together are a good reason to look.

  • A sewage smell in the yard or near the foundation.
  • A soggy or unusually green patch of lawn along the line.
  • Slow drains throughout the house or repeated backups.
  • Sunken ground or a small sinkhole over the line.
  • A rise in pests or sewer gnats near a cleanout.
  • Wet spots in a basement or crawlspace that do not match rainfall.

How a camera inspection works

A plumber feeds a small waterproof camera into the line through a cleanout. The camera records video and a locator tracks its depth and position from above. When it finds a crack, a displaced joint or roots, we mark the spot on the ground and show you the video.

Our sewer inspection page describes the process, and sewer line repair covers what we do when a defect is found.

Repair choices once a leak is found

Options include digging and replacing the damaged section, lining the pipe from the inside or replacing the line with a trenchless method. The right choice depends on the pipe material, depth, location and the surface above it.

If roots are the cause, clearing them is only a short-term fix. Hydro jetting can remove roots and scale, and a repair prevents the regrowth.

What a good inspection report should show

After the camera work, you should receive a clear record. That means video of the defect, a note of how far from the cleanout it sits, the depth from the locator and the pipe material. With that, you or another plumber can plan the repair without repeating the search.

A report should also explain what the camera did not show. A line that is clean today can still have a joint that opens in wet weather. If anything looked marginal, a good plumber says so and describes what to watch for.

Keep the video. If you sell the house, a recent inspection is a useful record for a buyer, and if the city or an insurer asks questions, you have evidence of the line's condition.

What you can do before the plumber arrives

A few steps make the inspection quicker.

  • Find your cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the yard or near the foundation.
  • Clear a path and move anything that blocks access.
  • Note where the symptoms appear first, such as a basement drain or a yard patch.
  • Limit water use if a backup has started.
  • Have the date of your last drain cleaning ready if you remember it.

Sources for sewer pipe findings

Pipe material references include ASTM A888 for hubless cast iron and ASTM D3034 for PVC sewer pipe. South Carolina law requires a call to 811 before any dig, and repairs follow the IPC as adopted in the state.

What this means in Easley, SC

In Easley, the city handles the sewer main in the street, and the homeowner is generally responsible for the line from the house to the main. A camera shows which side the defect is on. If it is on the city side, report it to the city. If it is on your side, we repair it.

Older Easley streets near downtown have clay and cast iron lines that are prone to roots and joint separation. South Carolina's 811 service marks underground utilities for free before any digging, and you should call it before a repair. If you are on septic outside the city, a leak in the line to the tank is a different repair, so tell us what you have.
